How it works

One memory's journey

An agent writes something down. Follow it from that moment to the point where a colleague in another office can read it — and see exactly where it could have been stopped.

The five stages

What happens, and what would happen if it went wrong.

  1. 1

    An agent writes something down

    A memory, a fact it worked out, a convention you corrected it on, or a skill it just installed. Any agent — Hermes, Claude Code, opencode, Codex, Copilot — because doublegate sits where its memory provider normally sits.

    Without a gate: that content is searchable the instant it lands, and it shapes every future answer until somebody notices it was wrong.

  2. 2

    The client gate holds it

    It goes into a separate database that your agent has no read path to. Not a flag on a row — a different store. Then three things happen to it:

    • Scanned — prompt injection, credentials, executable payloads in skills
    • Graded — by a reviewer running in its own process, with no tools and no network, that did not write the content
    • Signed — the verdict is recorded with a cryptographic signature

    If it fails: it stays held, with its reasons attached. It is not deleted and it is not silently dropped — it can be corrected and re-submitted.

  3. 3

    Visibility opens in two steps

    You are not blocked while review happens. Your own agents use it straight away — you wrote it, you already trust it. Your group reads it once signed, with the signature attached so they can see who vouched for it.

    Why this matters: a gate that makes you wait is a gate people route around. The delay only applies to other people reading your work, which is exactly where the risk is.

  4. 4

    The organization gate reviews it again

    Anything heading company-wide is submitted upward in the background and reviewed again on its own authority — a fresh judgement, not a rubber stamp of the first one. It is scored, given a second signature, and the decision is recorded with who, when and why.

    This is the second gate, and it is the one that makes company-wide sharing defensible. One authority approving its own submissions is not independent review.

  5. 5

    Every other gate picks it up

    Cleared content is distributed to every client gate in the organization — a teammate's laptop, your build runner, a gate in another office. It arrives pre-approved: their gate does not re-review what yours already proved, because the signatures travel with it.

    The compounding bit: one correction, reviewed once, reaches everyone. Today that same wrong assumption gets fixed separately in five hundred private agents, or not at all.

Where a claim goes, and what happens when it meets one already there

The two stores are two separate databases, not two shelves of one — which is why “nothing unapproved is readable” is a property you can check rather than a filter somebody has to remember to apply. A claim that matches something already held does not overwrite it: consolidation proposes a new claim and sends it back through the same gate.

What this costs your agent at read time

Nothing. That is the point of checking on the way in.

Reads are ordinary reads

Once content is approved it sits in a normal store and is searched like any other memory. There is no reviewer in the read path, no per-query approval check, no added round trip. Your agent does not know a gate exists.

Records are never held up

Transcripts, tool output and logs pass straight through — they are records of what happened, not claims about the world. Only claims wait for review, which is a small fraction of what an agent writes.

In our own measurements an agent produced roughly ninety times more records than claims. Gating everything would be unaffordable; gating only claims is cheap. Those numbers come from our machines, so treat them as illustration rather than a population finding.
