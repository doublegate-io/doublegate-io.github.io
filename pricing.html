Deployment and pricing

Reviewed knowledge,
scoped to who it serves

Four scopes with explicit authority boundaries. The organization gate is private and proprietary. Paid tiers provide shared review, identity and operating controls. The outermost scope is free and points outward: knowledge your organization chooses to publish.

Solo arrives with release 2 in Q2 2027, Team and Organization with release 4. The tiers below are the model; per-tier figures are set with the first organization deployments.

Solo

One developer, one machine

Self-hosted on your hardware, using local or hosted reviewing models you choose.

  • Review before your agent can act on what it wrote
  • Skills scanned before they reach your skill directory
  • Every agent on the machine shares one approved store
  • Signed local history you can export
  • Grows into Team by adding one shared gate — the store and its signatures move across as they are

Solo use is free, and stays that way.

most teams start here

Team

A squad or a department

One gate for the group — a shared runner, or each machine reporting to it.

  • Everything in Solo
  • Approved knowledge shared within current scope and access grants
  • Separate folders per project or client — a query in one never returns another’s content — connected only where you wire them
  • Reviewers who are never the author, enforced
  • New joiners receive reviewed knowledge within their assigned access
  • A review queue someone actually owns, with a daily digest
  • Grows into Organization by adding a second gate — the double gate itself, one tier up

Per active contributor, monthly. Self-host or managed.

Organization

Company-wide, multiple teams

Team gates submit upward to one or more organization gates you control.

  • Everything in Team
  • Independent second review before anything goes company-wide
  • Revocable organization-gate access, with read keys restricted to named folders
  • Exportable audit trail for regulators and internal audit
  • Planned erasure tooling, subject to deployment validation
  • Your own reviewing models and rules, not ours
  • Agents can report what their folder is missing — and propose the fix

Annual, by organization size. Runs in your infrastructure or ours.

free, and outward-facing

Commons

Anyone, publicly

A hosted gate for knowledge published beyond one organization.

  • Skills, memories and knowledge artifacts anyone can draw on
  • Wire a public folder into your own — every arrival reviewed on arrival
  • Nothing readable until a reviewer that did not write it has signed it
  • Published verdicts — including the rejections, with their reasons
  • Signatures travel with an artifact, so it arrives already vouched for
  • Contributors keep attribution; a bad approval can be traced and withdrawn
  • Arrives pre-reviewed; your own gate still gives it the second opinion it is designed for

Free to read, no account needed. Contribution under a named identity with a key.

Why the tiers are drawn where they are#

The boundaries follow the trust model, not the price sheet. That is deliberate, and it is checkable.

Solo — one reviewer, one beneficiary

You get the scanning and the signed history. You do not get sharing, because there is nobody to share with. That is most of the value, which is why this tier is free rather than crippled.

Team — one gate's authority

A group defines one gate’s review policy. Reading an approved revision still requires current access and an applicable scope.

Organization — a second authority

A second, independent gate reviews on its own judgement before anything crosses between teams. That second authority is the double gate — the tier boundary and the architecture are the same line.

Commons — the boundary moves outward

Your gate signs, the commons gate countersigns on its own authority. Exactly the Organization mechanism with the trust boundary one step further out. Reading is free; operating the commons still requires review inference and moderation.

A team can start on Solo and grow into Organization without migrating anything — same store format, same signatures, same export. Growth adds gates; it does not replace the engine. Publishing to the Commons is the same act as promoting between teams, aimed at a wider audience.

Where it fits, and where it does not#

The mechanism is for teams whose agents accumulate knowledge that outlives one conversation and one person. Three questions settle it.

Who needs the knowledge next?

The writer can recall a contribution after its security scan clears, marked as pending review. Sharing with others waits for admission. For personal memory without shared review, compare mem0, Zep or Mnemosyne.

Private reuse after scanning; shared reuse after admission

Who owns the review policy?

Choose human-led, model-assisted or automatic promotion under your rules. A named operator still handles scanner findings and cases requiring human judgment. Routine approvals can proceed automatically when the configured checks pass.

Automation with accountable exception handling

Does the knowledge need to outlive the person?

Lessons tied to one engineer's workflow stay fine in that engineer's tool. Knowledge a second agent, a new hire, or another team will rely on — that is what the company inherits, and what this is for.

company-owned knowledge → this is exactly the job

Solo serves personal use. Team and Organization add reviewed distribution, shared policy and responsibility for the exceptions.
