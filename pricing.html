Deployment and pricing

The same engine,
scoped to who it serves

Four scopes, one mechanism. Each tier is the same open-source engine — what you pay for is the shared gate, the identity model, and the operational surface around them. The outermost scope is free and points outward: knowledge your organization chooses to publish.

Solo arrives with release 2, Team and Organization with release 4. The model below is what we are building toward, not a price list you can buy from today.

Solo

One developer, one machine

Runs entirely on your hardware. Nothing leaves it.

  • Review before your agent can act on what it wrote
  • Skills scanned before they reach your skill directory
  • Every agent on the machine shares one approved store
  • Signed local history you can export
  • No shared gate — nothing to redistribute to

Open source. Free, and stays that way.

most teams start here

Team

A squad or a department

One gate for the group — a shared runner, or each machine reporting to it.

  • Everything in Solo
  • Approved knowledge shared across the team automatically
  • Reviewers who are never the author, enforced
  • New joiners inherit the team's reviewed knowledge on setup
  • A review queue someone actually owns, with a daily digest
  • Single gate — no cross-department policy

Per active contributor, monthly. Self-host or managed.

Organization

Company-wide, multiple teams

Team gates submit upward to one or more organization gates you control.

  • Everything in Team
  • Independent second review before anything goes company-wide
  • Who may read what, by identity and team
  • Exportable audit trail for regulators and internal audit
  • Deletion that is real, for erasure requests
  • Your own reviewing models and rules, not ours

Annual, by organization size. Runs in your infrastructure or ours.

free, and outward-facing

Commons

Anyone, publicly

A hosted gate for knowledge published beyond one organization.

  • Skills, memories and knowledge artifacts anyone can draw on
  • Nothing readable until a reviewer that did not write it has signed it
  • Published verdicts — including the rejections, with their reasons
  • Signatures travel with an artifact, so it arrives already vouched for
  • Contributors keep attribution; a bad approval can be traced and withdrawn
  • Not a substitute for your own gate — treat it as reviewed intake

Free to read, free to contribute. Reading it needs no account.

Why the tiers are drawn where they are

The boundaries follow the trust model, not the price sheet. That is deliberate, and it is checkable.

Solo — one reviewer, one beneficiary

You get the scanning and the signed history. You do not get sharing, because there is nobody to share with. That is most of the value, which is why this tier is free rather than crippled.

Team — one gate's authority

A group agrees to trust one gate's verdicts. Everything it signs is readable by everyone on it. One authority, one trust domain.

Organization — a second authority

A second, independent gate reviews on its own judgement before anything crosses between teams. That second authority is the double gate — the tier boundary and the architecture are the same line.

Commons — the boundary moves outward

Your gate signs, the commons gate countersigns on its own authority. Exactly the Organization mechanism with the trust boundary one step further out — which is why the commons costs nothing to run alongside a paid tier.

A team can start on Solo and grow into Organization without migrating anything — same store format, same signatures, same export. Growth adds gates; it does not replace the engine. Publishing to the Commons is the same act as promoting between teams, aimed at a wider audience.

When not to buy this

  • If agent memory must be readable the instant it is written. Checking on the way in is the wrong design for that requirement. mem0, Zep and Mnemosyne serve it well.
  • If nobody will own the review queue. An unowned queue becomes a backlog, and the gate becomes friction with no payoff. This needs a person, not just a licence.
  • If you want a productivity number in the business case. We do not have one and will not invent one. If the case has to be quantified in output terms today, we cannot help you make it.
