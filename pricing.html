Deployment and pricing

Solo is free, and stays free.
Team is on the roadmap.

Solo is free and self-hosted. Team adds one gate a group shares.

One gate runs today, and it serves Solo. Team has no price yet: it is set with its first deployments.

Solo

One developer

One machine, self-hosted, with the grading model the operator chooses.

  • Every agent on the machine shares one store of promoted claims.
  • Every claim is scanned and held for a reviewer, a person or a grading model, never its author.
  • The gate’s web console on that machine, and the Claude Code plugin.
  • Each session gets its own work area, where recall, the gate’s search, starts.
  • A signed journal of every decision, which anyone holding it can verify.
  • Session transcripts kept as evidence, outside recall.
  • Skill files the skill watcher finds in folders the operator keeps are reviewed like any claim and can be promoted.
  • Imported skill bundles are scanned and held, never promoted on this gate, and count toward its limit of 10,000 held claims.

Solo use is free, and stays that way.

Team

A squad or a department

One gate a group shares over the network, on the roadmap.

  • One review policy for the group.
  • The operating controls a group needs.

Priced per active contributor, monthly. Self-hosted.

Why the tiers are drawn where they are#

The tiers follow whose review the agents rely on: on Solo, one machine’s gate, and on Team, one gate a group shares.

Solo — one machine, one gate

The scan, review by someone other than the author, the signed journal, and one store every agent on the machine shares.

Team — one review policy for a group

A group sets one review policy for the gate it shares. Reading a promoted claim needs read on the space that holds it.

Both tiers use the same store format and the same signatures, so a group can grow from one machine without replacing anything. The name doublegate describes the next boundary: a second, independent gate that countersigns before knowledge crosses between groups. It is on the roadmap after Team.

Three questions that decide whether it fits#

doublegate is for organizations whose agents build up knowledge that outlives one conversation and one person.

Who needs the knowledge next?

Every claim waits for review before other agents can recall it, because a shared store distributes a mistake as readily as a fix. Its author can recall it as soon as its scan clears, marked held.

The author’s recall after the scan, other agents’ after promotion

Who owns the review policy?

The operator chooses who reviews: a person, a grading model or both. By default one grading model reads each claim twice, once for it and once against, and those two readings can promote it with no person. Nobody can clear a finding about secrets, access or integrity, whichever the operator choose.

One setting decides when a person must sign

Does the knowledge need to outlive the person?

Lessons tied to one engineer’s workflow stay fine in that engineer’s tool. Knowledge a second agent, a new hire or another group will rely on — that is what the company inherits, and what doublegate is for.

Knowledge the company inherits → reviewed once, kept for the next agent

Solo serves one person’s agents on one machine. Team adds the same review across a group, under one shared policy.

What it costs to run#

Running it has four costs: the machine, the grading model’s usage, reviewer time, and storage. Usage is hardware for a local model, fees for a hosted one. Storage only grows, because nothing is deleted.
