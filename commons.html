The Commons · planned for release 5

A shared library of agent knowledge
where nothing arrives unvouched

A planned public library of reviewed skills and reusable agent knowledge. Contributions will carry a named contributor, a publishing organization and review reasons, including refusals. Reading is free; reuse follows each artifact's published terms. Your team still checks whether a contribution fits its own environment and intended use.

The role the registries deliberately left open#

Not because nobody thought of it. Because the registries that hold this content decided, deliberately, that reviewing it was somebody else's job.

The canonical registry excludes curation on purpose

The official registry for the standard agent-tool protocol states as a design principle: "No preferential treatment for specific servers or organizations. No built-in ranking, curation, or quality judgments. Let consumers make their own curation decisions." A separate principle commits it to "avoid features that require constant human intervention or moderation."

They are right to. Moderation is an operational cost, and neutrality is a governance commitment curation would break. What they built is an index — and their own ecosystem document says curation belongs to "subregistries [that] add value […] by providing curation." That role is the one this page describes.

The registry's published design principles and its ecosystem vision, for the subregistry role

The most mature registry scans, then hands you the decision

Hugging Face runs pickle-import and antivirus scanning over uploads, and says of it: "Disclaimer: this is not 100% foolproof. It is your responsibility as a user to check if something is safe or not. We are not actively auditing python packages for safety." Their recommended mitigation is to load only from "users and organizations you trust."

Scanning is table stakes; a verdict is not on offer. That is not a criticism — it is the honest limit of automated checking at scale, from the team with the most experience of it.

And the measured result in agent skills

Of 3,984 public agent skills surveyed, 36.8% carried at least one security flaw and 13.4% a critical one. 76 were confirmed malicious, 8 still live at publication, and 91% of the malicious ones paired prompt injection with conventional malware. The barrier to publishing one is a file and a git repository.

This is the status quo the Commons is aimed at — not a hypothetical risk. The community's own guidance is to "validate the source yourself," which does not scale to anybody.

How publishing outward works#

Review and permission are separate. Local or team approval does not publish to the Commons; public release needs an explicit nomination, publishing rights and the receiving gate's review.

Four scopes — separate authority at each boundary
Solo you, on your own machine Free — and stays free

Your local gate reviews knowledge for your own use. Imports are external proposals, and local approval grants no permission to publish them outside your machine.

Team a group sharing one gate The shared gate

The shared-team design records the verified contributor and one accountable team at submission. Reviewers are not the author; changing teams retains original attribution.

Organization many teams, one authority A second, independent gate

The organization gate is private and proprietary. It applies its own authority to knowledge arriving from teams and can refuse it. Internal approval and an exportable review record do not authorize public disclosure of private claims.

Commons anyone, outside your company Read free, contribute signed

Your organization explicitly nominates a revision it has rights to publish. The planned Commons gate reviews it on its own authority; publication includes decisions and reasons in a form that protects private evidence and personal information.

Wider reuse requires review, current access and publishing permission
◈

You choose what leaves

Nothing is published by default. An organization nominates the artifacts it is willing to share. Check ownership, third-party terms, confidentiality, secrets and personal data before nomination. A generic topic alone does not establish publishing rights.

◫

Your gate signs it first

The selected workflow starts with a non-author AI trust assessment from 0–100, with evidence and reasons. A distinct authorized non-author AI or human then returns APPROVE or REJECT, without a second score. A high score never approves a contribution by itself.

⊘

The Commons gate reviews again

The planned public gate applies its own review and publication checks to the nominated revision. The two review stages are distinct from the local-to-public authority boundary. Ordinary review can be AI-only; human participation follows the applicable policy.

⎔

It arrives pre-vouched

Signatures travel with the artifact. Someone installing it sees who wrote it, who reviewed it, when, and on what basis. Approval means production-ready for the declared scope, version and use under required checks, not factual certainty or permission to execute.

Four properties planned for public artifacts#

Nothing is relaxed because the audience is wider. If anything the opposite — a wrong artifact here reaches everyone who installs it.

PropertyWhat it means in the Commons
Audited Publish decisions, including rejections and their reasons, under the applicable privacy and disclosure rules. Public accountability does not require publishing private evidence.
Traceable Each artifact links to its author, the organization that vouched for it, and the evidence behind it. When something turns out to be wrong you can find everything else from the same source.
Versioned A correction is a new version with a link to what it replaced. Nothing is edited under a reader's feet, and an artifact you installed cannot silently become something else.
Discoverable Searchable with provenance attached, so the search result tells you who stood behind the answer rather than only what it says.

Contributors keep attribution; ownership and reuse rights follow the artifact's terms. Public reuse grants no permission to export private organizational claims. Withdrawal and revocation must end eligible use within the service and retain a record of the decision. Erasure tooling is allocated to release 4; Commons delisting and privacy procedures still need operating proof. Destroying service-held keys cannot recall copies already downloaded.

Challenges and appeals keep decisions accountable#

The Commons governance commitment is settled: publish versioned criteria before opening, provide an appeals path with a human accountable for wrong refusals, and record outcomes. Staffing that path and proving public operation remain release-5 work.

The criteria are published before the gate opens

Criteria changes apply to future submissions and preserve the original signed decision. New counterevidence can still challenge an accepted artifact. Changed context triggers re-evaluation; required holds or withdrawal stop affected use rather than rewriting history.

A person answers for a wrong refusal

The planned appeal reaches a person; this does not require humans to review every submission. A signed outcome records whether a refusal was upheld or overturned, with publishable reasons. A successor revision needs its own review, not inherited approval.

Why an organization would contribute#

A shared supply of reviewed knowledge, with explicit costs and rights.

Reviewed intake beats free intake

The reason to contribute is the pool you draw from. Public artifacts that arrive already reviewed, with signatures attached, give your team evidence to evaluate instead of starting from an unexamined file. Local review, integration and maintenance still cost time.

Publish what you have rights to share

Common-tool guidance can be useful to others when you own it and choose to share it. Review its business value, publishing rights and privacy before release. Private workflows, evidence and claims stay inside their authorized audience; contribution is optional.

The business case rests on the supply line, not on goodwill. Contributing may well help your recruiting, your brand and your models; all three are unmeasured, so none of them belongs in your numbers.

Treat it as reviewed intake, not as trusted input#

The planned Commons publishes reviewed revisions, not universal truth. Receiving teams check scope, version, current validity and access before use. A signature remains historical evidence after expiry, challenge or revocation; it does not preserve permission indefinitely.

Run it through your own gate and budget for that review. Public provenance helps you investigate a disputed artifact; it does not establish suitability for your workload. Hugging Face states the limit directly: its scanning “is not 100% foolproof.”

Release 3 covers the selected review workflow and calibration; release 4 covers shared team controls; the Commons follows in release 5. Public operation still needs measured false-alarm rates, queue capacity, a staffed appeal path and abuse-resistance evidence. Identified contributions support accountability but do not solve identity-farming or volume attacks. Publish measurements before making quality claims.
