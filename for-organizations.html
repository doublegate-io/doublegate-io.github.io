For organizations

You are buying the learning curve
once per employee

Your engineers teach their agents every day — a corrected assumption, a convention that only exists in someone's head, a hard-won workaround. All of it lands in one person's private store and stops there. You paid for it and you do not own it.

What you are actually buying

Three things, none of them a black box.

⎔

An engine, not a seat on ours

Open-source software that runs on your infrastructure — your laptops, your runner, your cloud. The store is a database you can open, export and back up. No data reaches us unless you point a folder at the public commons, which is free and optional.

⊘

A review your team controls

Checking runs on the inference you already pay for, under rules you set. Reviewers are never the author — enforced by the architecture, not by policy. Knowledge arriving from outside your company is graded as foreign until your scanners prove otherwise, and the grading only ever tightens. When a company-wide rule changes, you change it in one place and every gate inherits it.

◈

Scopes that match how you grow

One engineer runs it free, forever. A team shares one gate's reviewed store. The company adds a second authority when knowledge crosses departments. Each scope is the same engine — you add gates as trust widens, and nothing migrates.

Deployment in one line: free for solo use, per-contributor for teams, annual by size for the organization scope — each including the one below it, self-hosted or managed. First release Q2 2027; talk to us about where a gate fits before then.

Why nobody pools it today

Because one wrong memory would reach everyone, and nobody could say who vouched for it.

Memory tools rank, they never block

Across fourteen providers we surveyed, a write is retrievable immediately. Confidence scores and decay tiers change ranking — never visibility. Pool that across a company and you pool its mistakes at the same speed as its insights.

What changes

Four outcomes individual AI licences do not deliver at any seat count.

◈

New hires start paired, not blank

A joiner's agent arrives already holding the reviewed conventions, patterns and corrections your team accumulated — signed, sourced and current. They inherit the last two years instead of rediscovering it.

◫

Knowledge stops leaving with people

What a senior engineer taught their agent over eighteen months is normally lost on their last day. Once reviewed it belongs to the organization — still attributed, still auditable, no longer dependent on them.

⊘

One correction, everyone benefits

Today the same wrong assumption gets corrected separately in five hundred private agents. Review it once and it propagates — the fix compounds instead of repeating.

⎔

An answer for "why does it believe that?"

Every belief resolves to a signed record: who vouched for it, when, and on what evidence. Exportable, so a regulator or an internal audit can verify the chain without going through you.

Getting the knowledge to the agents that need it

Review makes knowledge safe to share. Distribution is what makes the sharing cheap — and it works in both directions: out to every team, and in from the collections your company already curates.

Every collection lives in its own folder

Your platform team's conventions, the payment-rework project, the client engagement under NDA — each is a separate folder with its own review rules. A query in one folder never returns another folder's content, so confidential knowledge stays where you put it.

Folders connect — on your terms

You wire folders together yourself. When a team's folder publishes something approved, every folder connected to it receives it — reviewed again by the receiving folder's own rules, with nothing silently duplicated and nothing overwritten. The connection delivers; your review decides. And what any folder draws on is a query: every connection is listed, so a team’s whole knowledge supply chain is visible on demand.

the AI-first part

Agents that know what they are missing

Any agent — or a person on its behalf — can ask what its folder is missing: topics its queries keep failing to find, and knowledge a connected folder already holds. The system proposes exactly which reviewed items would fill the gap and waits for a person to act on it. Distribution stops being a guess about who needs what.

Knowledge that sharpens skills

When reviewed knowledge corrects something a skill teaches — a convention that changed, an edge case the skill gets wrong — the skill itself gets a revision proposal, citing the knowledge that motivated it. The revision is reviewed like any skill before anything changes, so what your agents execute keeps getting more precise without getting less trusted.

Both proposals — the gap report and the skill revision — are the same mechanism the rest of the product runs on: a second party reviews it before it counts.

One person's lesson becomes the whole company's

Reviewed knowledge that today reaches one engineer reaches the team and the organization, and every approval carries a signature you can check back to whoever made it.

Redistribution and provenance are properties of the architecture, verifiable from the first write. The productivity numbers — onboarding time, cost per correction — come from the first deployments; we publish measured figures, not projected ones, and the measurement plan is on the evidence page.
