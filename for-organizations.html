For organizations

You are buying the learning curve
once per employee

Your engineers teach their agents every day — a corrected assumption, a convention that only exists in someone's head, a hard-won workaround. All of it lands in one person's private store and stops there. You paid for it and you do not own it.

This has been measured

Not a thought experiment. Two independent findings, one month apart.

66firms

gave workers AI tools in a large-scale field experiment. Researchers found individual time savings and no broader shift in the quantity or composition of work.

Per-seat AI licences produce per-seat gains. They do not become organizational capability on their own, because nothing carries what one person learns across to anyone else.

World Economic Forum, September 2026
Metabuilt it

An "organizational second brain" with adversarial review by an agent that shares no context with the author, validation that passes or fails rather than scoring, human sign-off, and a complete audit trail.

They state they had not seen that level of validation rigor applied to an institutional knowledge base elsewhere, and they call the result "compounding institutional memory". A company with more engineers than most of their customers built this rather than buy it, because there was nothing to buy.

So why doesn't everyone pool it already?

Because one wrong memory would reach everyone, and nobody could say who vouched for it.

Memory tools rank, they never block

Across fourteen providers we surveyed, a write is retrievable immediately. Confidence scores and decay tiers change ranking — never visibility. Pool that across a company and you pool its mistakes at the same speed as its insights.

Our survey of fourteen memory providers

And your engineers install skills

Of 3,984 public agent skills surveyed, 36.8% carried a security flaw and 13.4% a critical one. Among the malicious ones, 91% paired prompt injection with conventional malware. Publishing one requires a file and a git repository.

Snyk, ToxicSkills

That risk is what doublegate removes — and removing it is what makes the knowledge worth collecting in the first place. The gate is the enabler, not the product.

What changes

Four outcomes individual AI licences do not deliver at any seat count.

◈

New hires start paired, not blank

A joiner's agent arrives already holding the reviewed conventions, patterns and corrections your team accumulated — signed, sourced and current. They inherit the last two years instead of rediscovering it.

◫

Knowledge stops leaving with people

What a senior engineer taught their agent over eighteen months is normally lost on their last day. Once reviewed it belongs to the organization — still attributed, still auditable, no longer dependent on them.

⊘

One correction, everyone benefits

Today the same wrong assumption gets corrected separately in five hundred private agents. Review it once and it propagates — the fix compounds instead of repeating.

⎔

An answer for "why does it believe that?"

Every belief resolves to a signed record: who vouched for it, when, and on what evidence. Exportable, so a regulator or an internal audit can verify the chain without going through you.

What we will not tell you

Every vendor in this space has an onboarding-cost figure and a productivity multiple. We do not, because we have not measured them.

  • No onboarding-time reduction. The figures circulating are vendor-blog statistics of unverifiable provenance. We will publish ours when we have run it.
  • No productivity multiple, no headcount equivalence. Nobody has measured what shared reviewed memory does to output. Anyone quoting a number is guessing.
  • No accuracy claim for the checking yet. How often review is wrong in either direction is unmeasured until we publish a false-alarm rate.

What we can defend: reviewed knowledge that today reaches one person can reach a team and a company, and every approval carries a signature you can check. That is redistribution and provenance — both verifiable, neither requiring a productivity study.
