A claim can be approved, revised or withdrawn#

The shared-team release pairs every claim with a verified contributor and a required team at submission. Teams follow the knowledge they maintain; contributors retain attribution as people and responsibilities move.

  1. Submit: a payments engineer proposes “retry with the original idempotency key” for one service’s timeout-retry procedure, citing an incident review. This is an illustrative scoped convention.
  2. Assess: stage 1 AI assesses trust from 0–100 using authorized instructions and the revision’s evidence. This assessment is not an approval vote.
  3. Decide: stage 2 is a distinct authorized AI or human, separate from both author and stage-1 reviewer, issuing APPROVE or REJECT with reasons, not another score.
  4. Revise or withdraw: a later incident changes the rule. A new revision carries the new evidence and contributor attribution through review; approval can supersede the old revision. Withdrawal ends eligible use while preserving its decision and reason. Rejection leaves the proposal outside eligible knowledge.

Two-stage review and withdrawal are planned for release 3; shared team attribution and publication for release 4. These are release plans, not a claim that the workflow is available today.

Approval covers a declared version, scope and use. A correction starts a new review; withdrawal ends eligible use and retains the reason. Team and contributor views follow the reader's access grants, including the counts and relationships they show.

Start with one team and a repeatable evaluation#

Choose repeatable engineering tasks and compare independently verified task success, total cost and maintenance effort with your existing workflow. Include failed and unfinished tasks, reviewer assistance and preparation work. Expand only after the evidence supports it, with explicit authority, access grants and receiving-team review rules.

Budget for inference, integration, policy upkeep, access administration, upgrades and recovery. Define success and stopping criteria before starting; retaining your existing workflow is a valid result.

Operational evaluation begins with release 4: time to a first independently verified role-relevant task and cost per correction, compared with similar participants and tasks using the same tools and source access, with and without reviewed shared knowledge. Correctness, assistance, failures and preparation effort accompany elapsed time. Published results carry the method, sample, uncertainty and deployment conditions; their timing depends on the evidence collected, rather than the release date. Getting the knowledge to the agents that need it#

Planned shared distribution and access controls: release 4. Skill revision proposals: release 5.

Review establishes what each team admits. Distribution carries that knowledge — and it works in both directions: out to every team, and in from the collections your company already curates.

Every collection lives in its own folder

Your platform team's conventions, the payment-rework project, the client engagement under NDA — each is a separate folder with its own review rules. A query in one folder stays within that folder. Organization-gate access is granted separately, with revocable keys restricted to named folders.

Folders connect — on your terms

You wire folders together yourself. When a team's folder publishes something approved, every folder connected to it receives it — reviewed again by the receiving folder's own rules, with nothing silently duplicated and nothing overwritten. The connection delivers; your review decides. And what any folder draws on is a query: every connection is listed, so a team’s whole knowledge supply chain is visible on demand.

the AI-first part

Agents that know what they are missing

Any agent — or a person on its behalf — can ask what its folder is missing: topics its queries keep failing to find, and knowledge a connected folder already holds. The system proposes exactly which reviewed items would fill the gap and waits for a person to act on it. Distribution stops being a guess about who needs what.

Knowledge that sharpens skills

Planned for release 5

When reviewed knowledge corrects something a skill teaches — a convention that changed, an edge case the skill gets wrong — the skill itself gets a revision proposal, citing the knowledge that motivated it. The revision is reviewed like any skill before anything changes, so what your agents execute keeps getting more precise without getting less trusted.

Both proposals — the gap report and the skill revision — are the same mechanism the rest of the product runs on: a second party reviews it before it counts.
