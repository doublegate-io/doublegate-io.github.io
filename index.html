Every correction your engineers make, every hard-won convention, every skill they install — it lands in one person's agent, stays there, and leaves when they leave. doublegate reviews what your people teach their agents — and what your existing systems can tell them — signs it, and turns it into a knowledge base the whole organization inherits. What is worth sharing further can go to a public commons on the same terms.

    What you get

    Four guarantees, and the mechanism behind each one.

    ◫

    Nothing unreviewed is readable

    Content under review lives in its own database with no read path from your agent. The boundary is structural, so it holds even when a query forgets to ask.

    ⊘

    The reviewer is never the author

    Review runs in its own process, without tools or network. An agent cannot sign off on its own work — approval always comes from a second party.

    ◈

    Approved once, useful everywhere

    You keep working while review happens. Your own agents use a memory immediately, your group gets it once signed, and the organization can clear it for everyone. Agents can even report what their folder is still missing.

    ⎔

    An audit trail you can export

    Every approval and rejection is signed and permanent — who trusted what, when, and on what basis. Hand the export to an auditor and they can verify the chain themselves.

    The trade, stated up front: checking costs tokens, and a new claim is not readable by others until it is signed. That gap is when the checking happens — an ungated store has no gap, and no check. What that costs in practice →

    Built for organizations where knowledge has to cross teams

    Three signs you are the reader this was designed for. Any one of them is enough.

    ◈

    Many teams, many agents

    Hundreds of engineers, each with an agent that learns something every day. The platform team's conventions, the payments team's workarounds, the on-call team's fixes — all of it useful to the others, none of it reaching them.

    ⊘

    Knowledge that must be defensible

    Regulated, audited, or simply large enough that a wrong belief propagating to five hundred agents is an incident. The question "why does it think that?" needs an answer with a name and a timestamp on it.

    ⎔

    Systems that already know things

    A core platform, a warehouse, a ticketing system holding what agents keep rediscovering. Knowledge management that has outgrown the wiki and needs a path into the tools people actually use.

    Knowledge management with a supply chain: what one team proves reaches every other team reviewed, signed and attributed — cross-pollination without cross-contamination. The business case →

    And a commons, on the same terms

    The outermost scope is free and points outward: skills, memories and knowledge artifacts anyone can draw on, where nothing is readable until a reviewer that did not write it has signed it — and every verdict is published, including the refusals.

    The registries that hold this content today left that role open on purpose. The canonical registry for agent tooling commits itself to "no built-in ranking, curation, or quality judgments" and expects curation to come from elsewhere. Hugging Face scans uploads and states plainly that it is "not 100% foolproof." Meanwhile 36.8% of 3,984 surveyed public agent skills carry a security flaw.
