What you get

Four guarantees, and the mechanism behind each one.

◫

Nothing unreviewed is readable

Content under review lives in its own database with no read path from your agent. The boundary is structural, so it holds even when a query forgets to ask.

⊘

The reviewer is never the author

Review runs in its own process, without tools or network. An agent cannot sign off on its own work — approval always comes from a second party.

◈

Approved once, useful everywhere

You keep working while review happens. Your own agents use a memory immediately, your group gets it once signed, and the organization can clear it for everyone.

⎔

An audit trail you can export

Every approval and rejection is signed and permanent — who trusted what, when, and on what basis. Hand the export to an auditor and they can verify the chain themselves.

And a commons, on the same terms

The outermost scope is free and points outward: skills, memories and knowledge artifacts anyone can draw on, where nothing is readable until a reviewer that did not write it has signed it — and every verdict is published, including the refusals.

This does not exist today for a documented reason. The canonical registry for agent tooling commits itself to "no built-in ranking, curation, or quality judgments" and expects curation to come from elsewhere. Hugging Face scans uploads and states plainly that it is "not 100% foolproof." Meanwhile 36.8% of 3,984 surveyed public agent skills carry a security flaw.
